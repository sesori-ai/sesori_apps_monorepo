The pinned prompt halo: how strong?

Option B is settled — a halo made of the page's own background colour. That is not up for discussion again. This page is only about its strength. You said the committed version reads as “literally a solid block”, and you were right to flag it. Round 1 is at the earlier page.

Two things were wrong, and one of them was my renders

1. The mockups you saw were not what the app draws

Flutter's widget-test framework sets debugDisableShadows = true by default. Every BoxShadow is then painted with no blur at all — just its spread, as a hard-edged rectangle. So round 1's images drew the halo as a 28 px-radius solid plate with a cliff edge and no fade whatsoever. It was literally a solid block, exactly as you said. The real widget has always blurred it.

Every image on this page is rendered with that flag turned off, so what you see below is what the app actually paints. I am sorry you were asked to judge a picture the app never draws.

2. The committed values are still wrong — just differently

With the blur restored, 28 / 14 is not a plate. Its problem is reach: the wash carries far enough to visibly grey out a transcript line a full row and a half below the bubble — a line the bubble never touches and has no business dimming. That is the halo becoming a visible shape.

So your instinct was correct on both renders. It is too intense; the reason is reach, not a hard edge.

The trade, stated honestly

The halo exists for one job: the bubble's edge cuts through a transcript row, and the bottom halves of those letters keep showing. In the fixture that row is “Older vaults keep their recorded iteration count, so”, sliced by the bubble's lower edge. The halo's job is to make that rubble go away.

Less reach means a prettier, more invisible halo and more rubble left showing. More reach hides the rubble and starts dimming rows that should be left alone. There is no setting that does both perfectly; the goal is the point where the rubble is gone and you still cannot see the halo as a shape.

Two numbers below, measured off the actual pixels rather than my eye. Rubble hidden is how much of the sliced row the halo erases. Neighbours left alone is how untouched the next full row down stays, where the weakest option (A) is the 100% mark because it barely reaches at all.

Two stacked shadows: tried, and not needed

You allowed a pair if it genuinely beat every single shadow. I rendered one — a wide blur 40 under a tight blur 12, spread 2 — and it does cover the rubble well (84%), but it leaves the next row at only 91% — G covers essentially as much rubble (80%) while leaving that row at 96%. A single blur 30, spread 8 was worse on both counts (58% / 87%).

One shadow wins, so the committed code keeps one. Short blur with real spread beats long blur every time here, because the rubble sits within about 20 px of the bubble and everything past that is collateral.
